# Title block

## Abstract

Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) affects over 10% of people aged ≥70 years and confers a five- to ten-fold increased risk of myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) and acute myeloid leukaemia [1]. No approved therapy targets CHIP-to-MDS progression or the bone-marrow microenvironment, yet recent single-cell studies implicate stage-specific stromal states — a senescent TNF-α/IL-6-producing state in clonal haematopoiesis [2] and an interferon-driven inflammatory stromal programme in lower-risk MDS [3] — that converge on a tonic TNF-α/IL-6 cytokine tone with TGF-β1-mediated fibrotic remodelling. We therefore hypothesize that chronic tonic inflammation of the marrow niche acts as a modifiable selective pressure on pre-malignant clones, and that chronic semaglutide — which suppresses NLRP3/NF-κB-driven inflammation and de novo TGF-β1 production in receptor-bearing stromal cells, with weight-loss-independent early effects on hsCRP [4] — will slow or reverse the annual increase in clonal variant-allele frequency (VAF) in adults with documented CHIP or lower-risk MDS and elevated inflammatory burden. This is consistent with observational signals of reduced AML transformation in lower-risk MDS during GLP-1RA therapy [5], which remain hypothesis-generating given residual confounding. Six pre-specified criteria define falsification, with explicit kill conditions: (i) effect magnitude scaling with baseline inflammatory burden; (ii) restriction of benefit to lower-risk disease; (iii) biomarker precedence, with hsCRP/IL-6/TGF-β1 and stromal-senescence signatures declining before any VAF change; (iv) receptor dependence, abolished by exendin(9–39); (v) mechanistic single-cell readouts favouring an indirect stromal mechanism, since no published study demonstrates a direct semaglutide effect on human haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells; and (vi) a stromal-restricted YAP-epistasis probe. The expected signal is niche biomarker changes preceding VAF-slope flattening over 12–24 months, at a magnitude calibrated to senolytic stromal depletion (~1.3-fold), maximal in high-inflammatory-burden carriers with a near-null negative control and no benefit in blast-dominant disease; conversely, a sharp early VAF decline would argue against the niche mechanism. This framework provides pre-registered, falsifiable criteria for a semaglutide trial in the pre-malignant marrow. No clinical recommendation is made; nothing here constitutes guidance for individual patient management.

## 1. Introduction

Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) — the clonal expansion of somatic variants such as *DNMT3A*, *TET2* and *ASXL1* mutations in the absence of haematologic disease — is present in more than 10% of adults aged over 70 years and confers a five- to ten-fold increased risk of myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) and acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) [1]. The progeny of a CHIP clone are phenotypically normal on every standard test, so the state is detectable only by targeted or genome-wide sequencing of blood or marrow, and it often goes undiagnosed altogether. Although overt MDS now has risk-stratified treatment options (erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, luspatercept, lenalidomide for del(5q) disease, hypomethylating agents in higher-risk disease, with allogeneic transplant the only curative approach), no approved therapy is directed at preventing progression from CHIP to MDS, and none targets the microenvironmental drivers of that transition. The therapeutic gap therefore lies in the pre-malignant window, during which a small clone may still be contained before cooperating mutations render it transformation-competent. This hypothesis identifies that window, and the niche within it, as the intervention target.

The strongest candidate for a modifiable driver in that window is not the clone itself but its environment. In ageing haematopoiesis, chronic low-grade inflammation — inflammaging — culls the stem-cell field: in younger adults, blood is produced by tens to hundreds of thousands of genetically distinct stem-cell clones, whereas in old age most blood is derived from as few as 10 to 20 dominant clones [6]. The "pre-clonal" and "clonal inflammaging" models [6] posit that this sterile inflammation creates a selective pressure favouring mutant stem cells, and the most direct human evidence that inflammatory tone *predicts* progression comes from a UK Biobank-based analysis in which elevated pro-inflammatory proteins predicted which CHIP carriers progressed to myeloid malignancy [6]. Recent single-cell studies have now located this pressure in the stroma and resolved it into two stage-specific states. In clonal haematopoiesis, mesenchymal stromal cells acquire a senescence-associated secretory programme driven by TNF-α and IL-6 from mutant haematopoietic cells acting through a STAT3-dependent pathway; depletion of these senescent stromal cells reduces mutant-clone burden and delays progression in mice [2]. In lower-risk MDS, by contrast, interferon-γ-producing T cells drive NF-κB-dependent inflammatory-stromal programmes that impair normal stem/progenitor output while preferentially sustaining mutant clones, a signature independently predictive of survival and leukaemic evolution [3][7]. The two states differ in cellular composition but converge on a tonic TNF-α/IL-6 cytokine tone with TGF-β1-mediated fibrotic remodelling of the marrow scaffold.

We therefore hypothesise that this tonic niche inflammation is a *modifiable* selective pressure, and we propose chronic GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonism as a testable probe of it. In the SELECT cardiovascular trial, semaglutide reduced high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) early and durably, with effects evident by 4–8 weeks and independent of weight loss [4]. Preclinical studies show that GLP-1R signalling suppresses NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome activity, with a shift of macrophages toward anti-inflammatory (M2) phenotypes, and GLP-1R expression on immune and haematopoietic cells is low in the same body of evidence [8] — consistent with an indirect, stromal route of action. The same receptor axis suppresses de novo TGF-β1 production in stromal cells, an effect abolished by the antagonist exendin(9–39) [9], and GLP-1R is expressed on bone-marrow mesenchymal stromal cells, where it promotes osteogenic differentiation [10]. To our knowledge no published study has demonstrated a direct effect of semaglutide on human haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells, consistent with the niche-targeted framing adopted here. Emerging clinical signals — most recently, a conference abstract reporting reduced AML transformation in lower-risk MDS during GLP-1RA therapy [5] — are hypothesis-generating and subject to residual confounding, but they justify formal testing.

The hypothesis, stated formally: chronic semaglutide in adults with documented CHIP or lower-risk MDS and elevated inflammatory burden will slow or reverse the annual rate of increase in clonal variant-allele frequency (VAF) by reducing low-grade TNF-α/IL-6 inflammation and TGF-β1-driven fibrotic remodelling within the marrow niche, without worsening cytopenias or accelerating transformation to AML. One scoping boundary is stated up front: obesity and type 2 diabetes co-occur with clonal haematopoiesis and share this low-grade inflammatory milieu [6]; the claim is not that metabolic disease *causes* haematologic malignancy, but that the shared inflammatory niche constitutes a modifiable selective pressure — which carries a falsification consequence, stated in Section 4: any benefit should scale with baseline inflammatory burden, not with glycaemic control or weight change. To avoid the common failure mode of a hypothesis that cannot be falsified, this manuscript states six pre-specified criteria with explicit kill conditions (Section 4) and an expected effect size calibrated to the magnitude of senescent-stromal depletion in preclinical models [2]. The framework is designed to falsify the hypothesis, including against our preferred outcome.

## 2. Inflammation as a modifiable selective pressure on pre-malignant clones

The inflammaging framework supplies a causal account of *how* inflammation selects for mutant clones: sterile, chronic low-grade inflammation of ageing — persistently elevated TNF-α and IL-6 activity with the senescence-associated secretory phenotype — precedes clonal outgrowth ("pre-clonal inflammaging") and is amplified by the mutant clone itself, whose progeny exhibit dysregulated innate immune signalling ("clonal inflammaging") [6]: a positive feedback loop in which inflammatory tone and clonal fitness mutually reinforce.

The account rests on converging preclinical and human evidence. In competitive murine models, an inflammatory environment containing TNF-α favourably selects *TET2*-mutant stem cells [13], and chronic inflammatory exposure acting through interferon pathways drives expansion of *Dnmt3a*-mutant clones [14]. The clinical analogue is the elevated frequency of MDS and myeloid neoplasms in chronic inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and vasculitis, reinforced by mechanistic work linking ageing-elevated inflammation to the expansion of individual CHIP mutations [15]. Two studies locate the causative inflammation *within* the marrow. First, VEXAS syndrome is a human natural experiment: somatic inactivating *UBA1* mutations restricted to myeloid cells drive an autoinflammatory, MDS-like disease in which massive type I interferon production makes clonal inflammation constitutive of the pathology [16] — the strongest demonstration available that tonic marrow inflammation and myeloid disease are mechanically coupled. Second, in a mouse model of age-related MDS progressing to AML, bone-marrow-confined IL-6 signalling was required for leukaemic transformation, and in human high-risk MDS marrow the IL-6 receptor is upregulated within the marrow compartment, where IL-6-pathway blockade reduced CD34+ clonogenic output [17].

Two honest boundaries. First, CHIP is initiated by mutation; no anti-inflammatory intervention repairs an altered stem cell — inflammation shapes which existing mutations expand, and can be expected to reduce, not eliminate, the growth advantage that mutant clones derive from their niche. Second, several of the cited models involve acute inflammatory exposures rather than chronic low-grade tone [13][14]; extrapolation from sepsis-like to inflammaging-like conditions is a genuine, if widely made, inference. The hypothesis therefore claims the narrower position that *chronic tonic* marrow inflammation is a modifiable selective pressure, and pre-specifies the experiments that would falsify this (Section 4).

## 3. Two stage-specific niche states

This section resolves the intervention target into two stage-specific niche states. The distinction matters for trial design: the states differ in cellular composition and demand different biomarker readouts; it is their convergence — not either state in isolation — that defines the modifiable pressure — pooling clonal-haematopoiesis carriers and lower-risk MDS patients without the distinction risks diluting the signal.

In clonal haematopoiesis the niche state is defined by mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC) senescence. In a *Dnmt3a*-mutant mouse model, single-cell profiling identifies MSCs in a molecular state of cellular senescence, induced in a contact-independent manner by mutant haematopoietic cells: soluble TNF-α and IL-6 activate a STAT3-driven pathway both necessary and sufficient for senescence induction. The state is not confined to mice — marrow MSC senescence is elevated in humans with clonal haematopoiesis driven by several common somatic mutations, arising without interferon-driven inflammation — and it is functional: depletion of senescent non-haematopoietic cells reduces clonal burden and delays progression to myeloid neoplasia [2]. Single-cell and spatial profiling of a large human cohort locates the same stage: clonal haematopoiesis is marked by the emergence of a distinct population of inflammatory mesenchymal stromal cells (iMSCs) alongside a concurrent loss of CXCL12+ adipogenic stromal cells [7].

In lower-risk MDS the niche state transitions: the iMSC population becomes more prevalent and CXCL12+ adipogenic stroma is lost further. In primary HSPC–MSC co-cultures, healthy aged and clonal-haematopoiesis HSPCs activate stromal support, but MDS HSPCs fail to do so and MDS blasts go further, suppressing HSPC support and triggering inflammation; interferon-responsive T cells preferentially interact with iMSCs, potentially reinforcing local inflammation [7]. This is the same interferon-driven programme in which interferon-γ-producing T cells drive NF-κB-dependent programmes in mesenchymal cells that impair normal stem/progenitor output while preferentially sustaining mutant clones [3]. Prummel and colleagues position the iMSCs as central mediators of early marrow-niche dysfunction and potential therapeutic targets for intercepting pre-malignant haematopoiesis [7].

The two states thus differ in cellular composition — a senescent, TNF-α/IL-6/STAT3-driven MSC state in clonal haematopoiesis versus an iMSC-enriched, interferon-responsive T-cell state in MDS — but converge on tonic TNF-α/IL-6 signalling and TGF-β1-mediated stromal reorganisation. It is this tonic, stromal-resident state, rather than either upstream trigger, that we propose is the modifiable selective pressure: it is carried by receptor-bearing stromal and immune cells rather than the mutant clone, and the GLP-1R axis suppresses precisely these inflammatory and de novo TGF-β1 outputs in stromal cells [9], so the target is accessible to an indirect, stromal-localised probe. ## 4. A mechanistic model and its six falsifiable kill-conditions

The central claim is indirect. Semaglutide acts on the niche, not the clone, for three converging reasons: GLP-1R expression is low on immune and haematopoietic cells [8]; the receptor is expressed on bone-marrow mesenchymal stromal cells, where it drives stromal (osteogenic) behaviour [10]; and the same axis suppresses de novo TGF-β1 output [9] and dampens NF-κB/NLRP3 inflammasome activity [8]. No published study demonstrates a direct semaglutide effect on human haematopoietic stem/progenitor cells. The model therefore places the drug on receptor-bearing stromal and myeloid-niche cells, reducing inflammatory cytokine and de novo TGF-β1 output [8][9] and, downstream, the senescent [2] or iMSC-enriched inflammatory-stromal state [7] that carries the pressure — so the pre-malignant clone loses part of its growth advantage and its variant-allele frequency (VAF) slope flattens.

The model's most characteristic, and most vulnerable, prediction is one of ordering: first to move, within weeks to months, are the circulating inflammatory and stromal outputs — hsCRP, IL-6 and TGF-β1 — and the stromal senescence/inflammatory signatures [2][7]; last to move is the clone's VAF, because losing a growth advantage is slow (12–24 months). A drug that flattens VAF early while those markers remain unchanged would implicate a direct clone mechanism — precisely the "sharp early VAF decline" kill condition in the Abstract.

Each of the six pre-specified kill-conditions maps onto a distinct break in the chain (Figure 2).

**(i) Effect scaling against baseline inflammatory burden.** A modifiable *selective* pressure implies dose–response to the pressure, not to the clone: benefit should track baseline hsCRP, IL-6 and stromal-senescence load and be near-null in low-burden carriers — the negative control that distinguishes the niche from metabolic drive (Section 1).

**(ii) Restriction to lower-risk disease.** Benefit is predicted where the clone still competes in an intact stroma — lower-risk MDS and CHIP — and none, or harm, where disease is blast-dominant, since the stroma, not the clone, is the target; MDS blasts suppress HSPC support and trigger inflammation, a state outside the modifiable window [7].

**(iii) Biomarker precedence.** hsCRP/IL-6/TGF-β1 and stromal-senescence signatures must decline before any VAF change (flattening expected over 12–24 months); VAF movement without biomarker movement identifies a direct clone mechanism.

**(iv) Receptor dependence.** Suppression of de novo stromal TGF-β1 by GLP-1R signalling is abolished by the antagonist exendin(9–39) [9]; the model therefore predicts a receptor-dependent stromal, and by extension clinical, effect.

**(v) Single-cell mechanistic readouts.** Single-cell profiling should show stromal-localised change — down-regulation of the senescent [2] and iMSC [7] programmes — without a direct semaglutide signal on mutant haematopoietic cells, consistent with their low GLP-1R expression [8]; a clone-localised effect would falsify the indirect framing.

**(vi) Stromal-restricted YAP-epistasis probe.** The model's most inferential step is that the modifiable stromal node routes through the canonical GLP-1R–YAP axis; a stroma-restricted YAP-inactivation probe tests it — if the stroma's anti-inflammatory response is YAP-dependent, the probe must abolish the predicted stromal benefit. If (vi) fails while (i)–(v) hold, the model narrows to a non-YAP stromal route — a refinement, not a falsification.

The model also encodes what its wrong versions would predict: a metabolic-driven benefit scales with weight or glycaemic change (refuted by i); a direct clone effect produces an early VAF change without stromal biomarker movement (refuted by iii and v); a clone-toxic effect worsens cytopenias or accelerates transformation. The expected effect is calibrated to the order of magnitude of senescent-stromal depletion in preclinical models [2] — stated as an order of magnitude, not an asserted fold.

## 5. Rationale for the probe: human and preclinical anti-inflammatory evidence

The Section-4 model is preclinical; it must be justified against the strongest evidence that chronic GLP-1R agonism in *humans* changes the very inflammatory tone the model targets.

**(5a) Human evidence (graded).** The anchor is a prespecified secondary analysis of the SELECT cardiovascular-outcomes trial, in which semaglutide reduced plasma high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) by 37.8% at 104 weeks in patients with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes; the reduction was evident by 4–8 weeks, occurred even among participants who lost no weight, and was independent of low-density-lipoprotein cholesterol and statin use [4] — the most direct human demonstration that chronic semaglutide suppresses a central output of the proposed tonic inflammatory state, independently of the metabolic route. The supporting cardiovascular-safety evidence is graded lower: in PIONEER-6, oral semaglutide was noninferior to placebo for the major-adverse-cardiovascular-events composite (hazard ratio 0.79; 95% CI 0.57–1.11) [18]; in SUSTAIN-6, subcutaneous semaglutide reduced the same composite in patients with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk (hazard ratio 0.74; 95% CI 0.58–0.95) [19]. Both were powered for cardiovascular safety and neither reported an inflammatory-biomarker endpoint; none enrolled clonal haematopoiesis or MDS, and none measured clonal variant-allele frequency — the clinical evidence that semaglutide alters clonal fitness is observational, not experimental, and is treated as such in Section 8.

**(5b) Preclinical mechanism.** The mechanistic link — NF-κB/NLRP3 suppression with an M2 shift, de novo TGF-β1 suppression abolished by exendin(9–39), and GLP-1R expression on marrow mesenchymal stromal cells [8][9][10] — is established and stated in Sections 1 and 4; it places the drug on the receptor-bearing stromal and myeloid cells, not on the mutant clone itself.

**(5c) Dose selection and population scope: the low-dose design, and what it rests on.** Two properties of the evidence above determine the Section-10 design, and both should be stated because they are where the design is either strongest or most exposed. First, the anti-inflammatory action of semaglutide is largely dissociated from the dose, and from weight. In the STEP 1, 2 and 3 trials, the 1.0 mg and 2.4 mg doses reduced C-reactive protein by similar magnitudes (−42% versus −49%; the between-dose difference did not reach significance, p = 0.06), and the reductions were consistent regardless of baseline bodyweight, BMI or glycaemic status [33]. An updated meta-analysis of 13 randomised trials (n = 26,131) found semaglutide lowered C-reactive protein (standardised mean difference −0.56 against placebo) irrespective of the treatment regimen or the population evaluated [34]; submaximal dosing is active and tolerated in real-world use, at magnitudes the weight readout shows in obesity [35]. This is the pharmacological basis for dosing for the inflammatory tone rather than for weight loss: at the receptor node, a low held dose is sufficient, and it is the version of the trial least exposed to the metabolic wrong-version that kill-condition (i) is built to refute. Second, the population scope: the population this design treats — adults with documented clonal haematopoiesis or lower-risk MDS, many of them of normal weight, and largely without type 2 diabetes — is the population the registered evidence has not covered. No published randomised trial enrolled normal-weight, non-diabetic adults, and the weight-loss-independent and low-dose-capable findings above were obtained in people with overweight, obesity, cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes [33][34][35]. That extrapolation is inferential, not established, and it is precisely the unoccupied node this paper names: the Section-10 design tests it in a population that carries the inflammatory state the model targets, so that a null or a positive result adjudicates a question the existing literature cannot. The two arms of Section 5 and this dose rationale converge on a single inference: chronic, low-dose semaglutide reduces the tonic TNF-α/IL-6 and TGF-β1 outputs of the marrow niche in a weight-loss-independent, receptor-dependent manner, and the population treated is the one in which that tone exists without the metabolic indications [4][8][9][33][34] — precisely the step the Section-4 kill-conditions are built to test, and why the probe is semaglutide, not a metabolic intervention.

## 6. Specificity control: why semaglutide, not metformin, anakinra or MCC950?

The Section-4 model is, at its core, a claim of specificity: the modifiable node is the stromal-localised, GLP-1R-dependent inflammatory and TGF-β1 axis. If that node were interchangeable, the probe could be any effective anti-inflammatory and the choice of semaglutide arbitrary. This section poses the additivity question against the three most plausible alternatives.

One caveat, so that the argument is not one of novelty: the only co-administration test of all three agents in a clonal-haematopoiesis model to date is a study of obesity-driven inflammation [20]. There, a UK Biobank cohort (n = 47,466; CHIP in 5.8%) linked clonal haematopoiesis to a higher waist-to-hip ratio, and in obese mice the calcium-channel blockers nifedipine and SKF-96365 — alone or with metformin, MCC950 or anakinra — suppressed CHIP-mutant-cell growth without isolating each agent's contribution. The modifiable-niche concept is thereby supported, but the effective agents are the calcium-channel blockers, the GLP-1R is not implicated, and the drug data are preclinical.

**(6a) Metformin — the metabolic alternative.** Metformin is first-line therapy in type 2 diabetes, which co-occurs with clonal haematopoiesis [6]; the human data do not support metabolic disease as a simple causal driver — the diabetes–CHIP association runs from clonal haematopoiesis to incident diabetes in prospective cohorts, consistent with a shared inflammatory milieu [11], and a Danish nationwide cohort of more than six million people found no association between type 2 diabetes (on the combined myelodysplastic-syndrome/acute-myeloid-leukaemia endpoint) or metformin use and the risk of developing myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukaemia [12]. Metformin's benefit is largely metabolic, its anti-inflammatory effects downstream of AMPK [21] — a probe of metabolism, not a receptor-specific stromal probe. A positive metformin trial would confirm the "metabolic" wrong version that kill-condition (i) is built to refute; metformin does not engage the GLP-1R, so it cannot deliver the receptor-dependent on/off (iv), the de novo TGF-β1 arm [9], or the stromal-osteogenic signature [10].

**(6b) Anakinra — the wrong node.** Anakinra is a recombinant interleukin-1 (IL-1) receptor antagonist with established efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis [22]. The specificity problem is that neither niche state is defined by IL-1 — the CHIP state is TNF-α/IL-6-driven through STAT3 [2], the MDS state interferon-γ/NF-κB-driven [3] — so anakinra is unlikely to relax the central programmes. It lacks GLP-1R engagement, de novo TGF-β1 suppression [9] and the stromal-osteogenic signature [10]; it appears in a preclinical CHIP combination regimen [20] but not through the receptor node this model targets.

**(6c) MCC950 — the strongest control.** MCC950 is a potent, selective NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor blocking IL-1β and IL-18 [23]. It is the closest mechanistic analogue of semaglutide's anti-inflammatory arm — the GLP-1R axis itself suppresses NLRP3 [8] — which is precisely why it is the key interchangeability control: if NLRP3 suppression alone reproduced the full niche benefit, the receptor-specific claim would be undermined. MCC950 nonetheless lacks the de novo TGF-β1 arm (a stromal output downstream of the receptor, not of NLRP3 [9]), the stromal-osteogenic signature [10], and human weight-loss-independent evidence — its human data concern autoinflammatory indications, notably gout. It tests the inflammasome node, not the receptor: a positive result would narrow the model to the inflammasome arm — a refinement, not a falsification.

**(6d) What the receptor-specific probe uniquely carries.** Four features distinguish semaglutide: a clean pharmacological on/off at the target node — abolished by exendin(9–39) [9] (kill-condition iv), a control none of the alternatives offers; the de novo TGF-β1 / fibrotic arm [9]; human weight-loss-independent proof of mechanism against the exact target — the SELECT hsCRP reduction [4]; and a safety record in older adults with cardiovascular comorbidity [18][19].

The alternatives differ from semaglutide in precisely the dimensions the model claims causal. If a future anakinra or metformin trial flattens VAF with the same temporal hierarchy, the niche hypothesis survives — the pressure is modifiable — but the probe-specificity claim (that the receptor is the node) is weakened; a semaglutide-specific effect would support the stromal-localised, receptor-dependent mechanism proposed here.

## 7. Stem-cell-side evidence and its limits: the HSC-iM "third voice"

One finding must be addressed honestly, because it places the inflammatory mark on the stem cell itself — the piece of literature most likely to be cited against a niche-targeted hypothesis. A 2026 *Nature* study developed xenograft inflammation–recovery models of human haematopoietic stem cells — shocked with TNF or lipopolysaccharide, recovery followed for months — and identified a state it terms haematopoietic stem cell inflammatory memory (HSC-iM): a durable transcriptional and open-chromatin signature of prior inflammatory stress, enriched in marrow from ageing donors and clonal-haematopoiesis carriers (mutant and wild-type cells alike), with clonal mutations attenuating the stress response toward activation and differentiation [24]. Read generously, this is a third piece of the inflammaging-as-selection puzzle — inflammatory exposure inscribed on the stem cell as well as the niche.

Read strictly, the foundation is more fragile than the headline suggests, for three reasons. First, terminology: what is demonstrated most closely resembles *trained immunity* — after an acute stimulus, myeloid progenitors and monocytes show enhanced inflammatory reactivity and open chromatin at NF-κB loci that persists for weeks to months and is lost with turnover, as documented in a BCG-vaccination study in which the state was carried from bone-marrow progenitors into circulating monocytes three months later [25] — reprogrammable, not inherited; HSC-iM, by contrast, is scored from a single recovery window plus correlation with age and clonal haematopoiesis. Second, and most important: for a chronic inflammatory state to drive clonal evolution over *decades*, the mark would have to be mitotically stable across many generations of division. No published study has performed the decisive test — lineage tracing, clonal barcoding or single-clone expansion in normal human cells — and trained-immunity marks wane over successive passages. The one normal-cell state with genuinely heritable open chromatin at inflammatory loci is cellular senescence, which loops back to the stromal state of Section 3 [2]. Third, the point that preserves the hypothesis: HSC-iM is built from acute, sepsis-like shocks, whereas the probe targets tonic, low-grade tone over months to years — a different input intensity; and if HSC-iM-like states matter at all, they are downstream of the same NF-κB/TNF-α/IL-6 tone the probe turns down, consistent with low GLP-1 receptor expression on haematopoietic cells [8].

The steelman, stated fairly: if "memory" requires multi-year inheritance, the authors have a hypothesis rather than an established fact, and their own durability evidence (weeks in mice; correlation with age and clonal haematopoiesis) sits inside the envelope that trained immunity documents for ordinary white blood cells [24][25]. (The circulating-blood mortality association reported alongside is a population-cohort inference, not prospective prognosis, and is treated accordingly.) Its strongest defensible claims — HSC-iM enrichment in aged and clonal-haematopoiesis marrow, and mutations altering how stressed stem cells respond — corroborate that inflammatory state is inscribed on the stem cell itself. The practical consequence: the stem cell and the stroma are not competing targets but two readouts of the same tonic inflammatory pressure, and a source-side probe remains the more testable lever because it acts on the input both depend on.

## 8. Human evidence and its discordances

Section 5 graded the evidence that semaglutide is a *plausible* anti-inflammatory probe; this section is the evidence that it is a *relevant* one — and the most discordant part of the case. The human data are observational, disagree with one another, and none measured clonal variant-allele frequency (VAF); they are hypothesis-generating, and Table 1 records the evidence level of each.

**(8a) Two discordant incidence signals.** The first is positive: in a TriNetX electronic-health-record cohort of ~1.6 million people with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1RA users had a lower risk of myelodysplastic syndrome than metformin users (hazard ratio 0.61; 95% CI 0.42–0.89), more marked versus insulin [26]. The second is null: a further multicentre TriNetX analysis found reduced multiple-myeloma risk with GLP-1RA use but no significant difference for myelodysplastic syndrome (hazard ratio 0.98) or acute myeloid leukaemia (hazard ratio 0.81); the agonist arm pooled several agents and carried no dose or duration data [27]. The analyses therefore disagree on the very outcome this hypothesis targets. The honest reading is that the incidence signal is suggestive but not yet stable — and, as Section 6 scoped, the [27] null is exactly what kill-condition (i) anticipates for a probe whose benefit should scale with inflammatory burden rather than with a categorical drug class. A single-arm observation cannot support the hypothesis; it can only fail to rule it out.

**(8b) A transformation signal, with an internal control.** The most clinically specific signal is a conference abstract reporting a meta-analytic propensity-matched analysis of 147,556 myelodysplastic-syndrome patients (Orland et al., *Blood*, American Society of Haematology 2025 — **a conference abstract, not yet peer-reviewed**). Among patients who started a GLP-1RA after diagnosis, transformation to acute myeloid leukaemia in *lower-risk* disease was lower across subtypes — most markedly with ring sideroblasts — and unchanged in high-risk disease; a parallel metformin comparison in the same cohort showed no effect in either risk group, an internal control against the effect of being given a diabetes drug [5]. As a conference abstract it is graded hypothesis-generating, and, addressing transformation rather than clonal burden, it is consistent with the niche hypothesis without testing it; a second propensity-matched analysis ran in the same direction for lower-risk disease but shares the same database and caveats.

**(8c) The confounders the signal must survive.** Every positive human data point is observational, and the bias class is specific: GLP-1 receptor agonists are actively *avoided* in frailty, severe gastroparesis and a history of pancreatic or thyroid malignancy, so users are pre-selected for better baseline health — a healthier-user and confounding-by-indication advantage that can mimic a protective effect. The analyses also share database and methodology, use unadjudicated ICD-derived myelodysplastic-syndrome codes, carry small absolute event counts, and — for the incidence studies — lack dose and duration data. The metformin internal control partly argues against pure healthy-user selection for the transformation outcome, but does not extend to the incidence studies nor distinguish a true anti-inflammatory effect from the residue of better metabolic care.

**(8d) What the human evidence does and does not show.** It shows the question is worth asking and the class is not evidently harmful in myeloid disease; it does not show that semaglutide alters clonal fitness. No trial enrolled clonal-haematopoiesis carriers and no analysis measured VAF, so the evidence for an effect on the clone itself is nil, and the inference from cardiovascular and transformation endpoints to a VAF endpoint is the hypothesis, not a result. The clinical case therefore rests on Section 5 (the probe's human anti-inflammatory properties) and the mechanistic model of Section 4 — precisely what the Section-10 trial is designed to resolve.

## 9. Expected effect size: what winning looks like, and what would argue against the mechanism

The expectation is deliberately conservative: the closest preclinical analogue — clearing senescent stroma in mice — is the one niche intervention with a directly measured effect on clonal burden [2], and it is modest: the order of magnitude to which the Abstract calibrates the signal. A realistic semaglutide effect is of that order, not larger.

Four features define a positive result, each mapped to a Section-4 kill-condition: **(a) Biomarkers move before the clone:** hsCRP first (apparent within weeks in SELECT [4]), then IL-6 and the stromal-senescence and inflammatory-mesenchymal signatures over months. **(b) The VAF endpoint is a slope, not a level:** a flattening of the annual rate of VAF increase over 12–24 months — a slower climb or a plateau — not disappearance of the clone within six months. **(c) The effect scales with the pressure:** largest in carriers with a high baseline inflammatory burden, near-null in inflammatory-negative carriers — the built-in negative control (kill-condition i). **(d) The effect is stage-restricted:** no meaningful movement in blast-dominant or higher-risk disease, where cooperating drivers — a *TP53* mutation, a high blast count — have taken over from the modifiable niche (kill-condition ii; Section 3 [7]).

Just as specifically, what would argue against it: a sharp early VAF decline preceding any biomarker movement would implicate a direct clone mechanism and falsify the indirect model (kill-condition iii) — a clone that turns over that quickly does not yield to a change in its environment. The handful of serial-VAF observations in which a clone shrank materially over months — including those in the Cleveland Clinic series — are the ceiling, not the expectation; a trial should not be powered on the assumption that they are typical [5].

Finally, what the hypothesis is, and is not: not a cure, and not a treatment for established high-risk MDS, where transformation risk is already dominated by cooperating drivers. It is a disease-modifying brake — it buys time, lowers incidence, and may hold a clone in "indeterminate potential" for years to decades. In a pre-malignant state whose only current therapy is surveillance, that is consequential even where the per-patient VAF change is modest — and precisely why the trial of Section 10 enriches for high inflammatory burden and pairs the VAF slope with biomarker trajectories, so the expected signal is detectable by design rather than by chasing the outlier ceiling.

## 10. Proposed study design: a pre-specified VAF-slope trial in the pre-malignant marrow

This section converts the Section-9 design requirements into a concrete, pre-specified study design: the study that would adjudicate the hypothesis, not a treatment proposal.

**(10a) Design and populations.** A prospective, randomised (registry-randomised acceptable), open-label study of chronic, low-dose semaglutide (0.25 mg subcutaneous weekly to begin, held at 0.5 mg; deliberately not escalated toward the 2.4 mg label maximum) versus usual-care surveillance in two linked cohorts: adults with documented clonal haematopoiesis (primary) and a nested lower-risk MDS cohort (IPSS-R very low/low). Open-label conduct is acceptable: the primary endpoint is objective and centrally read, blinded to allocation. The population is deliberately enriched for high baseline inflammatory burden (hsCRP, IL-6) and for the metabolic comorbidity in which the observational signals arose [26][27]; inflammatory-negative carriers form a built-in negative-control stratum (kill-condition (i); Section 9).

**(10b) Eligibility and exclusions.** Eligible: adults with documented clonal haematopoiesis (validated targeted NGS of peripheral blood) or lower-risk MDS with stable blood counts. Excluded: circulating or marrow blasts ≥5%, *TP53* mutation or a complex karyotype — adjudicated by a haematologist, because in them the transformation risk is no longer niche-limited (kill-condition (ii); Section 3); frailty, cachexia or unexplained weight loss; MEN2 or a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; pancreatitis; severe gastroparesis; pancreatic or thyroid malignancy; and planned standard-of-care therapy. The design presupposes a practising haematologist as co-investigator — and as co-author or formal correspondent — for eligibility adjudication and governance of the stopping rules.

**(10c) Endpoints and biospecimen schedule.** The primary endpoint is the change in the slope of clonal variant-allele frequency over 12–24 months — a slope, not a level (Section 9) — on the same laboratory, panel and platform for every serial measurement, with a centralised, blinded read. The endpoint has a direct design precedent in the longitudinal metformin/CHIP cohorts: typical *DNMT3A* clones grow at 5–7% per year, against which metformin's small, statistically significant change (−0.06% per year) is modest [28]. That null — metformin as the metabolic wrong-version control (Section 6) — fixes the expectation: any semaglutide signal is measured against the annual clonal baseline, not a hope of clone disappearance within months; sample size is set at protocol development against that benchmark and the expected effect magnitude (Section 9), explicitly not against the outlier serial-VAF observations [5].

The mechanistic readouts follow the Section-4 temporal hierarchy: plasma hsCRP and IL-6, and serum total and active TGF-β1 (with the active/total ratio), at baseline, 8–12 weeks and 6 months — the biomarkers must move first, before any VAF change, or kill-condition (iii) is engaged (the hsCRP effect is evident within weeks [4]); to our knowledge, serial TGF-β1 in that form has never been measured in a published GLP-1RA cohort — the panel is first-of-kind. A nested marrow-biopsy sub-study (baseline and ~6 months) tests whether GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy normalises not only de novo TGF-β1 synthesis [9] but also latent-pool activation, and supplies the single-cell stromal readout of kill-condition (v): the senescent [2] and iMSC [7] programmes must decline without a clone-localised signal.

Secondary endpoints: adjudicated MDS/AML incidence (event review, not ICD-derived codes — the Section-8 bias class), full blood count with differential, and safety — weight and nutritional status at every visit, gastrointestinal, gallbladder and pancreatic events, cytopenia stability. Weight change is a covariate, not an endpoint (kill-condition (i)); a 5–10% unintended weight-loss trajectory in a 70- to 90-year-old with MDS is a stop signal, not a success criterion.

**(10d) Comparator and specificity.** The comparator is usual-care surveillance. Receptor dependence (kill-condition (iv)) is adjudicated preclinically — the stromal TGF-β1 effect is abolished by exendin(9–39) [9] — and dissociated from a metabolic effect in the trial by pre-specified analyses against baseline inflammatory burden and weight/glycaemic change. A preclinical additivity test completes the specificity logic: in a low-VAF clonal-haematopoiesis mouse model, semaglutide co-administered with a short senolytic course (dasatinib–quercetin); an additive effect proves the GLP-1R route distinct from clearing senescent cells, full overlap shows the benefit routing through the senescent state [2]. The nested MDS cohort piggybacks on the first serial-VAF observations in this setting — the Cleveland Clinic series (conference abstract) [5].

**(10e) Safety oversight and stopping rules.** Oversight is independent of the treating team: a data safety monitoring committee reviews pre-specified stop rules defined before randomisation — worsening cytopenias, rising blasts, unexplained gastrointestinal symptoms, unintended weight loss of 5–10% or more with frailty markers, gallbladder or pancreatic events, and serious adverse events. Withdrawal is a legitimate outcome for a once-weekly injection that may run for years; monitoring continues past discontinuation, since a damped inflammatory tone may rebound after the last injection. The safety context is semaglutide's cardiovascular record in older adults [18][19]; the alternative niche strategy — direct senescent-cell depletion, the one intervention with a directly measured effect [2] — relies on agents limited by dose-dependent thrombocytopenia [29], a poor fit for a population often already anaemic or thrombocytopaenic.

The endpoints are cheap, standardised and already demonstrated in longitudinal metformin/CHIP work [28]; no new assay infrastructure beyond same-platform NGS, standard biomarker assays and optional marrow biopsy is required.

## 11. Discussion

**What the paper claims, and what survives a null result.** The central claim is not that semaglutide treats clonal haematopoiesis; it is the narrower, more durable claim that chronic tonic inflammation of the marrow niche is a modifiable selective pressure on pre-malignant clones, and that the niche — not the clone — is the intervention target. Semaglutide is the probe, justified independently at the receptor level (Sections 5–6). The reframing is practical: the contribution survives a null drug result — a failed trial still leaves the field a mechanistic model, two stage-specific niche states [2][3][7], a temporal-hierarchy prediction, and six pre-specified kill-conditions (Section 4) — a falsification architecture the observational signals [5][26][27] do not supply.

**The node is open.** The specific node this hypothesis points to — GLP-1 receptor agonism suppressing clonal-haematopoiesis-derived TNF-α/IL-6 tone, and thereby attenuating the stromal states that carry the selective pressure — does not appear in the papers that established its component parts, and, to our knowledge, no published study links GLP-1 receptor agonism to a marrow-niche mechanism in clonal evolution (a targeted reading of the cited literature, not a systematic audit). The kill-conditions exist precisely so that the node can be tested — and, if wrong, falsified — before the field converges on a single framing by default.

**Settled and inferential links.** The strongest layer is the stromal biology: the senescent MSC state induced by TNF-α/IL-6 through a necessary-and-sufficient STAT3 pathway, whose depletion reduces clonal burden and delays progression [2], cross-anchored by the iMSC-enriched, interferon-responsive state of lower-risk MDS [3][7]; and the receptor-level pharmacology — NF-κB/NLRP3 suppression, de novo TGF-β1 suppression abolished by exendin(9–39), low receptor expression on haematopoietic cells [8][9]. The inferential links, each with a designated falsification: (a) the route by which semaglutide reaches the human marrow niche — unproven in humans, addressed by the kill-condition (v) single-cell stromal readout; (b) the TGF-β1–fibrosis arm — de novo synthesis established [9], latent-pool activation an unoccupied question assigned to the nested biopsy sub-study (Section 10); (c) the extrapolation from the acute-inflammatory models that established selection [13][14] to the chronic tonic tone the probe targets — Section 2's boundary, corrected for by Section 10's months-to-years dosing. A fourth inferential link, the stromal YAP node, is held below, outside the testable model.

The open and untested nodes in the model — the inferential links (a)–(c) of Section 11 and the stromal YAP node held below — are tabulated in Table 3 (end-section).


**Speculative extension — not part of the testable model: the stromal YAP node.** Kill-condition (vi) designates the model's most inferential step: the claim that the stromal anti-inflammatory response routes, at least in part, through the canonical GLP-1 receptor–YAP axis. Its status should be held accordingly. The analogy on which it rests: the GLP-1 receptor is Gs-coupled, and stimulation of Gs-coupled receptors activates the Lats1/2 kinases and thereby constrains YAP/TAZ function [30]; if that branch operates in marrow stroma, GLP-1 receptor engagement would be predicted to constrain YAP/TAZ-dependent stromal output, including the inflammatory and fibrotic programmes the model targets. Two considerations argue against treating that branch as settled. First, the direction of regulation is contested within the marrow itself: in bone-marrow stromal cells, a Gs/PLC-coupled receptor signal (parathyroid hormone) engages a PLC/Ca2+/Src cascade that stabilises YAP through Y428 phosphorylation and promotes its nuclear translocation — the GPCR→YAP direction in marrow stroma is not settled [31]. Second, YAP/TAZ are dispensable for physiological and malignant haematopoiesis [32]; a stromal YAP effect, if it exists, should therefore appear in the stroma rather than in the clone, and a YAP-related effect localised to the clone should be read against the niche framing, not for it. The epistasis probe must accordingly be run against endogenous, wild-type YAP in a stroma-restricted system: an engineered YAP that resists Lats phosphorylation is not a valid substrate — a null there proves nothing. If the probe shows the stromal response to be YAP-dependent, the stromal node sharpens to a named mediator; if (vi) fails while (i)–(v) hold, the model narrows to a non-YAP stromal route — a refinement, not a falsification. Quantitative figures from this speculative arm are excluded from this manuscript pending verification against primary sources; the arm is deliberately held outside the formal hypothesis statement and can be spun off as a properly scoped study if the primary model survives.

**Reconciliation with the stem-cell side.** Section 7 treated HSC-iM and its limits leave the hypothesis intact; the Discussion adds the design-level resolution. The two arms of Section 10 separate the routes: the senolytic additivity test distinguishes preclinically a stromal-senescence route from a direct stem-cell route; and if HSC-iM-like states are real, they are downstream of the same tonic NF-κB/TNF-α/IL-6 input the probe turns down, consistent with low receptor expression on haematopoietic cells [8]. Stem cell and stroma are two readouts of one pressure; the probe acts on the input both depend on.

**Why now.** The node is open and the window for occupying it is: the mechanistic components are established [2][3][7][8][9], the human signals are being generated, if discordantly [5][26][27], and the endpoints are cheap, standardised and already validated in longitudinal clonal-haematopoiesis work [28]. For a pre-malignant state whose only current therapy is surveillance, writing the hypothesis down now — with predictions designed to be falsified against its own preferred outcome — fixes the framework before the field converges on a single one by default. A preprint at or before submission discharges the priority question for the authors; the Section-10 design discharges it for the field.

## 12. Limitations

The Discussion (Section 11) states what the paper claims; this section states the limits of the evidence — a hypothesis paper that omits its own weakest links invites the failure the Section-4 kill-conditions were built to pre-empt.

**(1) The human evidence base is entirely observational, internally discordant, and has never measured the endpoint the hypothesis targets.** All positive clinical signals are observational: the two incidence analyses disagree on the very outcome this hypothesis targets [26][27], share database and methodology, use unadjudicated ICD-derived MDS codes, lack dose and duration data, and carry small absolute event counts; no trial enrolled clonal-haematopoiesis carriers and no analysis measured VAF, so the inference from cardiovascular and transformation endpoints to a VAF endpoint is the hypothesis, not a result (Section 8).

**(2) The single most haematology-specific signal is a conference abstract** — reduced AML transformation in lower-risk MDS during GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy, with a metformin internal control — not peer-reviewed, not yet validated, and not trial evidence [5]. It is retained because it justifies the "field is moving" framing, and it is graded accordingly at every appearance.

**(3) The effect-size anchor is pending primary-source verification.** The single numerical anchor — the expected signal calibrated to the magnitude of senescent-stromal depletion, as stated in the Abstract [2] — has been verified against the abstract, not yet the full text; until then the effect size is an order of magnitude, not an asserted fold (Sections 4, 9), and the serial-VAF observations in which a clone shrank materially over months, including those in the Cleveland Clinic series [5], are the ceiling of what individual cases have shown — the design is not powered on them (Section 10c).

**(4) The metformin null anchors, and bounds, the expectation.** The longitudinal metformin/CHIP cohorts found a small, statistically significant change in the VAF-slope endpoint (−0.06% per year against typical *DNMT3A* growth of 5–7% per year) [28]; the pre-malignant window is not drug-permeable by default, so a negative trial result would be informative, not surprising.

**(5) Extrapolation boundaries.** The selection models involve acute, sepsis-like inflammatory exposures [13][14], not the chronic tonic tone the probe targets (Section 2's boundary, corrected for by Section 10's months-to-years dosing); receptor dependence (kill-condition iv) is adjudicated preclinically, with no human antagonist arm (Section 10d); the active/total TGF-β1 panel is first-of-kind, its interpretability in this population untested (Section 10c); and the "open node" statement (Section 11) is a targeted reading, not a systematic audit.

**(6) Design-level caveats.** The sample size is set at protocol development against the longitudinal benchmark [28] and the expected effect magnitude (Section 9), explicitly not against the outlier serial-VAF observations [5]; the study is open-label (primary endpoint objective, centrally read, blinded); VAF comparability over 12–24 months depends on same-platform assay stability; and adherence to a once-weekly injection that may run for years is itself an outcome under monitoring (Section 10e).

On current evidence the honest label is promising and unproven — which is exactly why it is stated with pre-specified kill-conditions rather than as a claim of efficacy, and why its primary claim is the modifiability of the niche: a claim that survives a null drug result (Section 11).
